# LinkedIn Content Scripts
5 ready-to-post scripts. Copy, paste, post. Each one is designed to attract your exact target client: international professionals who are stuck in the European job search.

---

## POST 1: The Insider Secret (short, high engagement)

I open CVs for European companies every day.

Here is what happens in the first ten seconds when an application comes in.

We look at three things:
- Does the job title or headline match what we are searching for?
- Is there any number or result in the first visible lines?
- Does the location or work authorisation make sense for this role?

If the answer to any of those is unclear, we move on.

Not because you are unqualified. Because there are 200 more to look through and clarity wins.

If you are sending applications and hearing nothing back, start there.

What does your CV say in the first ten seconds?

## POST 2: The Pain Point Story (longer, drives DMs)

Six months ago one of my clients had been applying for jobs in Europe for nine months.

She had a Master's degree. Seven years of experience. Strong English. Spoke three languages.

Zero interviews.

When she came to me, I looked at her CV and LinkedIn for about five minutes.

The problem was not her experience. It was that her CV was built for the US market and her LinkedIn headline said "Open to opportunities."

LinkedIn Recruiter does not search for "Open to opportunities." It searches for job titles and keywords.

She was invisible.

We rewrote her headline, updated her location to her target city, and restructured her CV in two pages with actual results and numbers.

First interview request: 11 days later.

If you are sending applications and hearing nothing back, the problem is almost always specific and fixable. It is rarely about your qualifications.

DM me "CV" and I will tell you what I think is blocking you.

## POST 3: The Myth Buster (short, shareable)

Things people believe about the European job search that are not true:

"I need to know someone to get hired." No. But you do need to be visible in recruiter searches.

"My CV just needs a light edit." Usually it needs a rebuild. Not because your experience is weak, but because the format and language are built for a different market.

"I need to apply to hundreds of roles." Ten well-targeted applications to the right companies will always beat 200 sent into the void.

"If I have not heard back in two weeks, it is a no." European hiring is slow. Three weeks of silence in Spain or Switzerland is normal, not a rejection.

The search that works in Europe looks different from the one that worked at home.

What is the one thing you were told about the European market that turned out to be wrong?

## POST 4: The Differentiator (positions you vs other coaches)

Most career coaches have never hired anyone.

They know how to write a CV. They know how to prepare you for interviews. That knowledge is useful.

But they cannot tell you what a recruiter in Zurich actually thinks when they open your application on a Monday morning. Because they have never been that recruiter.

I am a working recruiter. I fill roles for European companies every week.

When I work with a candidate on their CV or LinkedIn, I am not giving them theory. I am telling them exactly what gets an application opened and what gets it skipped. Because I make those calls daily.

That is the difference.

If you are searching for a job in Europe and want to know what is actually blocking you, message me. I will tell you what I see.

## POST 5: The Result Post (social proof, drives trust)

Last month a client came to me after six months of silence from the European market.

Senior Finance Manager. 12 years of experience. Three languages.

The problem: his CV listed his responsibilities from every role. Not one number. Not one result. Just a list of what his job description said he was supposed to do.

We spent two hours translating his experience into achievements. What he built, what he grew, what he fixed, and by how much.

New CV went out. First positive reply came back within a week.

He had not changed anything about what he did. Only how he described it.

If your applications are going out and nothing is coming back, that is usually where the problem is.

Your experience is real. The question is whether the way you are presenting it is working in this market.
